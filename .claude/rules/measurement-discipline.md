# Measurement Discipline — Calibration Choice + Cost Dimension + No-Cherry-Pick (P0 Diligence)

What makes the audit trail diligence-grade is not the measurements themselves but the discipline that surrounds them. The Phase 7.2 arc proved the discipline holds at $3.00 cost. This rule encodes what worked.

## When this rule applies
- Designing a new measurement (calibration choice, cost dimension, tie-break rules)
- Reporting a measurement result (especially when the result is unfavorable on the headline metric)
- Withdrawing or superseding a previously-claimed number
- Setting up an A/B comparison
- Deciding whether to label a number "conservative" / "permissive" / "calibrated to X"

## Calibration choice IS policy, not a tuning knob

Every measurement that involves judge calibration, threshold choice, or scoring rubric must:
1. Document the calibration choice at design time, before any results are seen
2. Preserve the calibration across runs — do NOT soften it post-hoc to make numbers look better
3. Label results with the calibration explicitly: "0.375 span correctness under conservative Sonnet calibration", not "0.375 span correctness"

Phase 3.9 conservative-judge calibration: the prompt defined SUPPORTS / NEUTRAL / CONTRADICTS thresholds before any spans were judged. The 0.375 N=10 POD batch result was reported with the calibration explicit. A post-hoc relaxation to lift the number above 0.5 would have fabricated quality.

## Cost dimension selection — explicit and consistent

When measuring cost-vs-quality, the cost dimension must be explicit and consistent across the comparison. Do NOT mix dimensions mid-Pareto.

Phase 6A used **per-query mean latency** as the cost dimension across all 21 configurations. Alternative dimensions (LLM tokens, dollar cost, GPU-seconds) would have been valid choices, but mixing them would have made the Pareto frontier non-comparable. Pick one and stick with it for the whole comparison.

Document the choice in the report header: "cost dimension = per-query mean latency (single-user wall clock)".

## Tie-break rules — declared in advance

When two configurations are dominated-equal on the primary metric, the secondary metric is the tie-break. Document the tie-break rule before seeing the results.

Phase 6A: pass rate primary, MRR secondary. `fts_vec_kg_k60` (production) and `fts_vec_kg_k30` both scored 92.0% pass rate; `k=30` won the MRR tie-break (0.6207 vs 0.5963). The tie-break being declared in advance prevented post-hoc rationalization ("k=60 is what we shipped, so let's prefer it").

## A/B methodology — control vs treatment, identical inputs

- **Identical inputs**: same gold set, same snapshot, same runner config
- **Deterministic where possible**: content-derived `run_id`s for FTS5 reproduce byte-identically; hybrid has chromadb tie-break non-determinism that affects rank ordering on non-passing queries but not headline pass rate
- **Regressions reported alongside lifts**: never report only the favorable side
- **No mid-comparison changes**: do not switch backends, snapshots, or gold sets between control and treatment runs

Phase 4 HyDE A/B was the canonical example: Run A (HyDE off) vs Run B (HyDE on), identical snapshot + gold set + runner config, both run records persisted with run_ids, regression measured at -10 pp, reported unsoftened with 5 regressions / 0 lifts in the headline.

## No-cherry-pick discipline

When a measurement produces an unfavorable result on the headline metric, the result is reported with the same prominence as a favorable one. Selectively reporting only the favorable number is the failure mode this rule prevents.

Phase 5 MRR-vs-pass-rate diagnostic is the canonical example: hybrid achieved +14 pp pass rate over FTS5, but **-0.0007 MRR**. Both numbers ship. Selectively reporting "+14 pp pass rate" without the MRR diagnostic would have hidden a real architectural finding (hybrid demotes some FTS5 rank-1 hits to rank 2-3 while adding new passing queries at deeper ranks).

The benchmark reports generated by `reporting/generate_report.py` enforce this — MRR + nDCG@10 are computed alongside pass rate in every report; the reader sees all three in the same table.

## Withdrawn-claim discipline

When a previously-claimed number cannot be defended under measurement, withdraw it formally. Do NOT silently let the unsupported claim continue to circulate.

Examples:
- **Phase 3.75-A**: the historical "82% hybrid retrieval" claim was retired in favor of measured 92.0% via the new evaluation harness. The retirement was documented in the closeout AND the achievement record AND the document state line — three independent surfaces carry the new number with explicit pointer to the prior claim being superseded.
- **Economic Impact PDF v2 → v3.1**: prior session work formally withdrew "<2% hallucination, 96% citation precision, 82% multi-hop" claims from v2 in favor of v3.1 with tiered-evidence framing. The withdrawal is annotated in the document; v2 is not silently replaced.

The withdrawal entry includes: the prior claim verbatim, the new claim verbatim, the evidence that supports the change, the date of withdrawal.

## Conservative-vs-permissive labeling

Every measurement that involves judge calibration or threshold choice must label which side it sits on. The label travels with the number into every downstream report.

- ✅ "0.375 span correctness under conservative Sonnet calibration on N=10 POD batch (179 claim-citation pairs)"
- ✅ "Cohen's kappa = 0.497 (moderate agreement) between Ollama gemma4:26b and Sonnet 4.6 on 25 (claim, evidence) pairs"
- ❌ "0.375 span correctness" (calibration unstated; reader cannot interpret)
- ❌ "moderate inter-judge agreement" (number stripped; reader cannot verify)

## Sample-size disclosure

Every measurement reports its `n`. When `n` is small, that constraint is part of the headline.

- Phase 3.9 N=10 POD batch — span correctness measured on 179 (claim, citation) pairs across 10 queries, NOT on the full 50-query gold set. The closeout, the achievement record, and the report generator's span-correctness section all carry this caveat.
- Phase 4 HyDE A/B at n=50 queries — the report explicitly notes "the generator does not do statistical significance testing on deltas; a +5 pp delta on n=50 may or may not be a real effect; the report shows the raw delta and lets the reviewer decide".
- Phase 6A Pareto sweep at 21 configurations × 50 queries — wall time scaled, but per-query latency stable across worker counts, so per-config comparisons hold.

When `n` is the load-bearing constraint, surface it in the same sentence as the headline metric. "92.0% pass rate (46/50)" is correct; "92.0% pass rate" alone is not.

## Provenance always present

Every measurement claim cites: gold-set name + version, corpus snapshot id, run id, runner config. Without these, the measurement cannot be reproduced and cannot be defended under diligence.

The benchmark report generator enforces this — every report header carries the four provenance fields, and the reader can re-derive the measurement from the same inputs.
